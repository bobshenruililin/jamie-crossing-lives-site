A life doesn’t move
all at once.

Home, work, services and relationships can reach across a border in different ways. Follow the choices people make, and the lives they keep connected.

Research by Jamie Ko · Visual production with Bob

12 sets of conversation and visit notes, with two separate expert discussions. An exploratory inquiry.

Four overlapping arrangements: cross for a service, move home, work or study across, or maintain ties elsewhere. Home, work, spending and relationships need not share a location.

01 / Livelihoods and choices

Selective choices.
Different pressures.

Specialist services, price and experiences attract people across. Time, convenience and crowding also shape what they choose.

Meals in Shenzhen/Shopping in Hong Kong

She judged lower living costs and the opportunity to guide to be a more workable balance. Her finances were not audited.

02 / Everyday services and navigation

Finding guidance.
Finding suitable services.

Crossing, settling in and maintaining daily routines involve practical information and services people can find.

Jamie’s Futian visit

Immigration staff directed Jamie to an arrival-form kiosk. Separately, staffed help points near the exit offered practical information.

What this project draws on

The research combines twelve mixed qualitative records, plus two separate expert discussions: eleven earlier conversation, observation and visit records, and one Sheung Shui visit on 31 October containing three brief chats. It is not a representative survey. The four life arrangements illustrate overlapping possibilities; they are not a population classification.

Official series, programme descriptions, evaluations and forecasts have different evidentiary roles. Each interactive section links to its sources and states the main limits. Causal claims need suitable comparisons.

The presentation and main story follow the revised script checked on 31 October 2026. Maps, policy documents and the research collections provide additional context. This is a working draft for review.

About the visuals

The optional route game uses invented demand, capacities and rules. The spatial model uses authored architecture and conceptual relationships, not measured routes, distances or journey frequencies.

Field photographs were supplied by Bob and Jamie. The Singapore–Johor visit and their Shenzhen field trip are kept distinct. Captions describe visible context; no pictured person is identified as a research participant.

The Futian help-point example is a field observation: its operator was not confirmed and described referrals were not tested.
