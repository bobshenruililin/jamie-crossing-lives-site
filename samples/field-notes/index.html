01 / THE CROSSING

A LINE, MANY ROUTINES

A border is a line
on a map. Life is
less tidy.

Home, work, family and services do not always sit in the same place.

02 / AN OBSERVATION
T

he crossing is easy to draw as a single line. On the ground, it becomes a sequence: a sign, a corridor, a wait, a decision about where to go next. Each belongs to a larger geography of ordinary life.

About this visual and its sources

This is a conceptual illustration of arrangements that may overlap, not a population classification. The Hong Kong–Shenzhen research and Singapore–Johor context involve different institutions and borders; one should not be treated as a prediction for the other.

The arrangement descriptions are drawn from the project research data. The Singapore–Johor question and its limits follow the project’s 31 October source review. The prose interlude is newly written for this visual sample, not a quotation or field observation.
